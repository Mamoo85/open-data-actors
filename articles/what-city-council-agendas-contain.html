What is in a city council agenda feed: 28 of 60 rows are procedure

We looked at 60 agenda rows for meetings between 2026-09-28 and 2026-10-08 in 2 cities. A row is called procedural when its title matches a short phrase list (call to order, approval of the agenda or minutes, adjournment, roll call, public comment, announcements and similar; the list is in the script). By that rule 28 rows (47%) are procedural.

Substance

16 rows link to a legislative matter file. Of those, 11 have an action recorded and 7 have a vote result. Matter types present: Information Item (Inf) (7), Appointment (Appt) (1), Approved Minutes (1), Proclamation (4), Resolution (3).

The practical point for anyone watching zoning, contracts or appointments: filter on matter-linked rows, not on all rows, or most of what you read will be housekeeping.

Limits

  • Two cities, one with a single meeting in the sample. Seattle and Denver publish differently, so the per-city split reflects the portals as much as the councils.
  • Whether an action or vote is filled depends on whether the meeting has happened and the clerk has entered it, so recent agendas look emptier.
  • The procedural phrase list is ours and will miss some lines and catch some others.

Data and method

Input: 60 rows from a test run of the city-council-agendas actor, stored as council_items.json with attachment links and note text removed (the public sample CSV has 12 rows). The figures on this page are computed by a3_council.py (run it from the articles/_data folder). Related: the actor page and its sample CSV. Published 2026-10-13. We build and maintain these actors.
